\title{It Takes an Agent to Build an Agent}
\subtitle{Building a Prostate Cancer Decision-Support Agent Without One}

\begin{abstract}
We entered the CHIMERA-agent challenge with two goals: to place well, and to learn how
an autonomous coding agent fares as the entrant. It was designed, implemented and
measured by an LLM agent (Claude Opus~5) directed by a human over 27 days, against an
audit trail of commits, pre-registered predictions and cross-validated A/B tests. Its founding plan specified a hybrid in which a
language model gathered evidence and wrote the rationale; a closed loop --- read the
released scorer, hypothesise, measure, let the measurement decide --- removed every
learned and generative component, leaving a system whose entire fitted object is
nine categorical labels and which runs no language model at inference. It scores
$0.8061$ on validation and beats a random forest out of fold. We report the method,
what measurement removed, the one thing it got wrong, and the five points where the
loop needed a human.

\keywords{Agentic software development \and Prostate cancer \and Reasoning traces
\and Model Context Protocol}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

CHIMERA-agent~\cite{chimera} sets three prostate-cancer tasks over multimodal
records from Radboudumc and Karolinska: whether to biopsy on MRI alone (Task~1), how
to treat given MRI and biopsy (Task~2), and time to biochemical recurrence after
prostatectomy (Task~3). Distinctively, the \emph{reasoning trace} is scored --- a
confidence level, a weighting of each clinical variable, a declared sequence of
record sections retrieved over the Model Context Protocol (MCP)~\cite{mcp}, and a
free-text rationale graded by a G-Eval-style judge~\cite{geval}.

\paragraph{Disclosure.}
The architecture, code, experiments and measurements here were produced by Claude
Opus~5 with shell and file-system tools over 27 days, directed by the author, who
reviewed the work, made the decisions in Sect.~\ref{sec:human}, and is sole author and
accountable party. Per COPE guidance a language model cannot be an author; it is
disclosed as a method, and named because the result is a claim about agent-driven
development.

\paragraph{The question, and the loop.}
The second goal made the process an object of study, and it ran as a closed loop:
read the released evaluator, hypothesise what it rewards, measure under
cross-validation, and let the measurement --- not the plan, not intuition about what
an agent \emph{ought} to look like --- decide what ships. The loop was not neutral
about its own conclusion. The founding plan of 8~August specified a ``hybrid
architecture'' whose free text came from ``a real MCP-driven LLM agent'', alongside a
decision-theoretic selector it calls ``the clearest source of edge''; of its six
proposed components, none ships (Table~\ref{tab:removed}). What remains decides by a
nine-row lookup and narrates from a template --- higher-scoring here, and the only
version a clinician can check without running a judge.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

Three parts apply in order to the structured patient card. A \emph{stratifier} maps
the case to a guideline leaf and looks up its decision; an \emph{acquirer} retrieves
over MCP the record sections that decision requires, appending each to a ledger; a
\emph{narrator} writes the rationale from that evidence, and \code{reveal\_sequence}
is emitted from the ledger. Nothing is learned end-to-end and no component is a
language model.

\paragraph{Stratification.}
Task~1 is stratified on PI-RADS~\cite{pirads} alone into \{\code{high} ($\geq 4$),
\code{low}, \code{unknown}\} --- a depth-1 stump. Task~2 uses an EAU risk
stratification~\cite{eau} over prior-biopsy status, ISUP grade, PSA and clinical
stage, into six leaves. Each leaf carries one fitted label, so \emph{the entire
learned object is nine categorical values}, chosen under cross-validation to maximise
the official metric; there is no per-case learned function, and naming the leaf
explains the prediction. Labels are fitted to the ranking metric, not accuracy: Task~1
ranks on $(\text{mean case score} + F_1^{\text{yes}})/2$, so a label trades accuracy
against the reasoning behind it.

\paragraph{Acquisition and reveal honesty.}
\code{reveal\_sequence} is self-reported and the evaluator never inspects MCP traffic,
so a declared retrieval scores whether or not it happened; we treat this as a
constraint, not an opportunity. Our MCP client appends every successful section read
to a per-case ledger and emits the declaration \emph{from} that ledger, so a section
can be declared only if a tool call returned it: reveal honesty is structural,
not a maintained convention, and we optimise which evidence to \emph{acquire}, not
what to claim. Client and server are hand-rolled over the standard library --- the
official SDK's dependencies do not fit the offline image --- and tested for
conformance against the reference client.

\paragraph{Narration.}
Two properties of the judge determine the rationale's wording, neither guessable from
the output schema. First, its evidence context is the narrative record sections,
\emph{not} the structured patient card, so a card value reads as invented unless a
report states it too: across the 348 released Task~1/2 cases PI-RADS co-occurs in the
reports 95--97\% of the time and ISUP 100\%, but prostate volume only 48--58\%, so the
templates quote the former freely and the latter never. Second, procedural meta-data
(``Guideline basis: \ldots'') reads as bookkeeping, not reasoning, so the stratum
opens the rationale as a clinical characterisation.

\paragraph{Task 3: ordering, not regression.}
Task~3 ranks on Harrell's C-index~\cite{harrell} alone, so only \emph{ordering}
matters and its trace earns nothing. We score each case with CAPRA-S~\cite{capras}
and one MRI tie-break, mapped monotonically to months: $\text{risk} =
\text{CAPRA-S} + 0.99\,p_{\text{csPCa}}$, out of fold $0.7373 \to 0.7522$. CAPRA-S
takes 12 integer values and ties 104 of 1130 pairs at $0.5$; the tie-break's weight is
a \emph{bound}, not a fit --- below~1 it reorders \emph{within} an integer band, never
across one, so it need only beat a coin flip and vanishes where a site emits none.
Everything richer we tried gains by reordering \emph{across}
bands, assuming one site's imaging model arrives calibrated like the other's --- the
trade we declined, and, once, did not (Sect.~\ref{sec:results}).

\section{The Loop, and What It Removed}
\label{sec:loop}


The loop's first pass was source-reading, not data. The released \code{evaluate.py}
shows the Task~1/2 case score to be a weighted sum of six components, only one of
which --- the rationale --- is generated text. Hence the hypothesis that most of a
scored reasoning trace is an annotation form rather than an act of reasoning, and so a
supervised target. Every later pass measured against it, and each removed something
(Table~\ref{tab:removed}).

\paragraph{The loop caught its own misreading.}
That founding reading was partly wrong --- grounding at $0.15$ and the rationale judge
at $0.10$, against verified $0.05$ and $0.20$, valuing the generative component at
\emph{half} its weight. Re-deriving reversed one shipped decision but not the
architecture: a conclusion surviving a twofold correction to its key input is worth
more than one never checked.

\paragraph{What removed the language model, honestly.}
Three forces converged and only two are measurement. (i)~The deterministic reasoning
components sit at their practical ceiling --- an in-sample bound under $+0.005$
overall, zero out of fold --- leaving little for a generative component to win back.
(ii)~The offline image could not carry the MCP SDK's dependency tree, let alone model
weights. (iii)~The plan gated the LLM writer on a question put to the organizers on
29~August and directed that the deterministic pipeline ship if no answer came before
validation opened; none came, and the component lapsed by that default. Measurement
made retiring the language model \emph{safe}; a deadline and an unanswered e-mail made
it \emph{happen} --- an agent's process has deadline-driven defaults able to decide an
architecture.

\paragraph{The tree the agent authored beats the forest it did not fit.}
The stratifier is a decision tree with an authored topology and fitted leaf labels,
which invites the obvious comparison. Under $6\times5$ repeated stratified
cross-validation, re-fitting the nine labels inside each fold so every row is out of
sample, the authored tree wins on both tasks and the random forest is runner-up on
neither (Table~\ref{tab:models}). Two reasons are instructive. On Task~1 a
\emph{fitted} depth-1 stump picks PI-RADS as its root on only 17 of 30 folds,
preferring \code{cspca} (8), \code{vol} (4) and \code{psad} (1) --- cardinality bias,
since four-level PI-RADS offers three thresholds where continuous PSA offers ninety.
On Task~2 a depth-3 tree nearly recovers the EAU structure ($0.8361$) but bagging
destroys it ($0.7222$): the rule is a sharp conjunction, and averaging 500 trees
smooths thresholds that should not be.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

Table~\ref{tab:val} gives the campaign, weighted $0.4/0.4/0.2$. Every slot changed one
thing and carried an interval committed beforehand, which is what makes the failure
below readable. S2 matters for \emph{how} it was validated rather than for its size:
we pre-registered $+0.046$ to $+0.057$ and realised $+0.0705$, \emph{above} the band.
Which cases moved shows the mechanism right and the magnitude wrong: exactly six
changed, all six had been wrong, all six moved to the ground-truth label, and the
per-case price was predicted to within 6\%. Only the leaf's prevalence was
underestimated --- three times commoner in validation (6/36) than training (4/72) ---
so a test cohort resembling training yields roughly $+0.024$; we regard that
projection, not the leaderboard delta, as the result.

\paragraph{The one time the fold was wrong.}
S3 and S4 spent two slots on a second Task~3 term, $+2\,(\text{PI-RADS}-2)$, unbounded
and so able to move a case \emph{across} CAPRA bands. Its warrant was a separation in
training --- all 19 recurrences at PI-RADS 4--5, none of the ten at 2--3 --- and out of
fold it was the only Task~3 change whose paired bootstrap excluded zero: $0.7522 \to
0.7965$, CI $[+0.0015, +0.1007]$. On validation it lost $0.157$ of C-index. S4 fixed a
real absence-handling defect, recovered $+0.116$, and still landed $0.041$ below the
term-free ordering: the pre-registered floor fired and the term was withdrawn. One
case carries the whole result --- a recurrence at 12.5 months, the cohort's earliest,
at PI-RADS~2, ranked 15th of 23; drop it and the term is worth $+0.080$, not $-0.041$.
The warning was legible in advance and we misread it: an interval that barely excludes
zero and is wide relative to its centre reports that few events carry the estimate,
not that the effect is established. Re-fitting the weight on the 23 validation cases
would have beaten our best submission by $+0.017$; the pre-registration forbade it,
and the sweep is non-monotone anyway --- pair flips on seven events, not an optimum.

The system is small: three dependencies installed \code{--no-deps}, no weights, 40
modules under 1\,MB, 263 tests covering MCP conformance, evaluator parity to
$10^{-9}$, and no outbound connection.

\section{Where the Loop Needed a Human}
\label{sec:human}

Every point at which the human changed the trajectory --- what a leaderboard number
hides. They fall into a pattern.

\begin{enumerate}
\setlength{\itemsep}{0pt}\setlength{\parsep}{0pt}\setlength{\topsep}{3pt}
\setlength{\partopsep}{0pt}
\item \textbf{Do not tune on the debug leaderboard.} Unmetered and returning per-case
components, it reads as free signal --- but its cases are our training data.
\emph{Ignored:} in-sample gains on twelve cases, carried into validation as if they
generalised.
\item \textbf{``Better model, or overfitting?''} on seeing $+0.0705$, which beat the
loop's own band and had been banked as success. \emph{Ignored:} no prevalence
decomposition, and the $+0.024$ projection above never computed.
\item \textbf{Do not chase Task~1's remaining $+0.049$}, the largest lever left and
visible in every metrics dump. \emph{Ignored:} its six failures are all false
negatives, and PI-RADS ${\geq}4$ is 69\% positive in training against 100\% in
validation, so fitting on training suppresses the calls the cohort rewards.
\item \textbf{Spend the remaining days on the manuscript, not the unused slots.} The
loop optimised score; the manuscript is the gate that makes score count.
\emph{Ignored:} no paper, and an entry not qualified for ranking. Adopted in part:
two slots still went to PI-RADS, for zero net score and a term withdrawn before test.
\item \textbf{``Is this a decision tree --- would a forest help?''} The standard
learned baseline had never been run in 27 days. \emph{Ignored:} no
Table~\ref{tab:models}, and the first reviewer to ask would have had no answer.
\end{enumerate}

Interventions 1--3 are one failure mode in three settings: \textbf{the agent optimises
the number it can see}. The loop reliably generated hypotheses and killed them on
evidence --- most of Table~\ref{tab:removed} is the loop refuting itself --- but the
prior that \emph{a visible gain is suspect until decomposed} came from the human every
time, and the PI-RADS term of Sect.~\ref{sec:results} is the same failure with no human
present, stopped by a pre-registered floor rather than by doubt. Intervention~4
optimises the stated proxy over the real objective, invisible to measurement because
the manuscript requirement is not in the metric. Intervention~5 is mildest and most
instructive: the loop tested its hypotheses rigorously and never generated the standard
baseline --- thoroughness within a frame is not questioning the frame.

\paragraph{A property of the metric, reported rather than exploited.}
\code{cost\_aware\_tool\_score} (weight $0.15$) is a precision, $|A \cap R|/|A|$,
returning $1.0$ when $A = \emptyset$: under-retrieval is never penalised and the empty
declaration is \emph{weakly dominant} --- an agent that reads nothing scores at least
as well as one retrieving exactly what the urologist did. Its intended counterweight,
\code{section\_grounding\_score}, carries only $0.05$ and is bounded; our scores show
tool $1.000$ against Task~2 grounding $0.223$, a floor rather than headroom. The rule
fixed at the outset --- choose the policy first, actually retrieve, declare from the
ledger --- forecloses the exploit, and we report the property rather than use it. The
fix is to score evidence \emph{acquired} rather than \emph{declared}: a server can
check it, and it costs an honest entry nothing.

\section{Limitations and Conclusion}

\paragraph{Limitations.} This is $n=1$: one challenge, one agent, one model, one human,
no control arm. The agent's contribution is not separable from the model's prior
knowledge of prostate-cancer guidelines or from the interventions above; the audit
trail makes the process inspectable, not controlled. The system is also underpowered
clinically --- Task~1's errors concentrate in prior-positive biopsy patients at high
PI-RADS, a subgroup near 50/50 that no model in Table~\ref{tab:models} separates.

\paragraph{Conclusion.} An agent asked to build an agent for a benchmark that scores
reasoning planned to use a language model and, over 27 days of measuring its own
proposals, removed one: most of a scored trace is an annotation form, higher-scoring
and auditable as a supervised target, and what needs real judgement is small and
mostly unlearnable here. But the one addition the folds endorsed --- the campaign's
only interval excluding zero --- was wrong, and a pre-registered floor caught it.
\emph{Decompose any gain that beats its own band} is the invariant we would mechanise
first.
